02 / The problem

Lafenwa · September 2026

She paid ₦1,000 every day for six months. Then the collector vanished.

Every morning the alajo came by her stall, collected ₦1,000 and ticked her card. Six months of ticks. ₦180,000 of pepper money, put aside for school fees in September.

Then one week he didn't come. His phone was off. His stall was locked. Nobody at the market knew where he went.

All she had left was a card. And a card is only paper. It tears, it gets wet, it gets lost, and the only other copy of the record was in his head.

Saving was never the problem. Proof was.

08 / Questions

What people ask us at the market.

Do I need a smartphone?

No. Savers get receipts by SMS on any phone, even a small Nokia. Only your alajo uses the AjoProof app.

Who is holding my money?

A licensed partner, not AjoProof and not the collector's pocket. Your alajo deposits each day's cash, and your receipt changes to Secured once the deposit is confirmed.

What if my collector stops coming?

Your record doesn't go with them. You can see every payment you made and how much was deposited, and we help you claim what is yours.

How does the loan part work?

After about three months of saving, you can ask for your savings history report and share it with a lender. They see how regularly you saved and how much. They never see it without your yes.

I'm an alajo. Do I change how I collect?

You still go round the market, still collect cash, still keep your people. The difference is that you type each payment into your phone instead of a notebook, and you bank by evening.

Does it work in Yoruba?

Yes. Receipts can come in Yoruba or English. Bẹ́ẹ̀ni, a ń sọ Yorùbá.
